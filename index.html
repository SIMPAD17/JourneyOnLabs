The apps

Draw it, then simulate it

Each app computes its results from your drawing as you draw, and each is verified against published exact solutions and benchmark cases. They are simplified two-dimensional models for learning; their results are estimates.

Sketch CFD

Sketch a shape, see the flow

Sketch CFD is an educational tool for learning computational fluid dynamics (CFD) from simple sketches: draw it, then simulate it.

  • See heat move the air
  • See gases mix
  • Measure it live
  • Photograph a paper sketch (Sketch CFD Pro)

Verified against the lid-driven cavity (Ghia, Ghia and Shin, 1982), flow around a cylinder (Schäfer and Turek, 1996) and a heated square cavity (de Vahl Davis, 1983).

14 examples and a wind tunnel are free. Pro adds the rest.

Sketch FEA

Sketch a part, see the stress

Sketch FEA is an educational tool for learning finite element analysis (FEA) and the mechanics of materials from simple sketches.

  • Watch a crack run through it
  • See how it vibrates
  • Find the buckling load
  • Compare with theory

Verified against a cantilever beam (Timoshenko and Goodier, 1951), a plate with a hole (Kirsch, 1898) and Euler's column (Euler, 1744).

Every example is free, and so is a steel or aluminum bar of your own; Pro adds the rest.

Sketch Heat

Sketch a part, see the heat

Sketch Heat is an educational tool for learning heat transfer from simple sketches.

  • Watch it warm up
  • Walls, frames and insulation
  • Radiation between surfaces
  • Aluminum, copper, and more

Verified against a plane wall, a cooling fin and a suddenly heated block (Lienhard and Lienhard, A Heat Transfer Textbook, 2024).

Every example is free, and so is a plate of aluminum or copper of your own; Pro adds the rest.

Sketch Fields

Sketch a part, see the field

Sketch Fields is an educational tool for learning electric and magnetic fields from simple sketches.

  • Flux through an electromagnet
  • See the field and capacitance
  • See current crowd at a corner
  • Every material, with its source

Verified against a parallel-plate capacitor (Ling, Moebs and Sanny, 2016), a coaxial cable (Ellingson, 2018) and a two-wire line (Boulé, 2024).

Every example is free, and so is your own capacitor; Pro adds the rest.
